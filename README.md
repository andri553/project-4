PT Sukses Pinjam Digital (PinjamAJA) is a fast-growing Indonesian fintech peer-to-peer (P2P) 
lending and micro-investment platform based in Jakarta. Over the past four years, PinjamAJA 
has successfully captured a massive market share of the "unbanked" and "underbanked" 
population across Java and Sumatra, boasting over 5 million active users. 
To maintain its market dominance and evolve into a full digital financial ecosystem, the Board of 
Directors has launched the "SatuNusa Expansion" initiative with two primary strategic targets: 
1. Cross-Border QRIS & Micro-Insurance: Expanding the app to support Bank Indonesia’s 
cross-border QRIS payments (for Indonesian tourists traveling in Southeast Asia) and 
launching integrated micro-insurance products. 
2. BPR Acquisition Integration: PinjamAJA recently acquired a small Bank Perkreditan 
Rakyat in Central Java to acquire a core banking license. The goal is to migrate the 
BPR's traditional savings accounts into PinjamAJA’s cloud-native architecture within 6 
months. 
Mr x as the new appointed CISO currently at a critical turning point. The enforcement grace 
period for Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Law (UU PDP) has ended, and regulators are 
actively auditing fintechs.  
Mr x initial assessment reveals the following critical gaps: 
1. Board of Directors and Board of Commissioners are hyper-focused on user acquisition. 
The CFO views security strictly as an IT expense. They heavily pressure the product 
team to keep the electronic Know-Your-Customer (e-KYC) process as brief as possible, 
even if it skips certain identity verification checks. 
2. The software engineering team uses agile microservices hosted on AWS and Google 
Cloud. Due to speed pressure, developers frequently hardcode API keys into public 
code repositories and deploy features without security testing. 
3. The acquired BPR runs its core banking on an on-premise legacy system with outdated 
Windows servers, unpatched databases, and weak physical access controls. 
4. The Otoritas Jasa Keuangan (OJK) and Bank Indonesia (BI) have issued warnings 
regarding PinjamAJA’s IT Risk Management posture. A major concern is the storage of 
highly sensitive customer data, including photos of KTP (ID Cards) and verification 
selfies, which are currently stored in unencrypted cloud buckets. 
5. Most of the employee (70%) is not IT people which is not familiar with term and IT 
environment.
